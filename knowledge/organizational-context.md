# Working principles for volunteer civic technology

**Discussion draft — not adopted policy.** Prepared November 3, 2026.
This is a workflow reference, not a current project roster, assignment of
authority, or service commitment.

## Start with people and needs

- Build with affected people rather than assuming a technical solution.
- Describe the problem, intended users, and evidence of need before choosing
  tools or promising delivery.
- Make room for research, design, writing, organizing, testing, and documentation
  as well as code. Do not make technical experience a prerequisite for belonging.

## Keep scope compatible with volunteer capacity

- Choose a small, useful next milestone with a clear way to tell whether it
  helped.
- Ask who has actually agreed to contribute or maintain the result. Do not
  infer a commitment from a name in an old repository.
- Record dependencies, uncertainty, and support expectations. A prototype is
  not a promise of an ongoing service.
- Pausing, narrowing, or handing off a project can be a responsible outcome.

## Reduce coordination and continuity costs

A short project brief can capture:

```text
Problem and intended users:
Evidence of need / user involvement:
Next small milestone / completion criteria:
People who have agreed to participate:
Dependencies / open questions:
Maintenance and handoff expectations:
Next review point:
```

Keep private personal information out of public briefs.

## Keep human decisions visible

Automation can prepare drafts, summarize approved public material, suggest
small tasks, and improve documentation or tests. Humans retain decisions about
priorities, commitments, consent, access, public statements, merges, and
external actions.

Before publishing a roster, event, partnership, outcome, or support commitment,
confirm it with the responsible people and an appropriate current source.
This document establishes no event cadence, legal status, named authority,
attendance figure, or current project state.
